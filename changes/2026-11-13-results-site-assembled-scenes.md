## 2026-11-13: The results site's Assembled view covers the wheel, the tripod plate and the knob, and plays each recorded fit (no result changed)

Dan, on the trial page's 3D view: "you could see it with the wheel on it, a different material, and the proper orientation", and "show the animation of it being pushed in. Also, if it can't get pushed in, and if it's too loose, it just falls out when you turn it over." Only the Estoa light box had an assembled view. No verdict, `grades.json`, `summary.csv` or grader changed.

- **`scripts/hardware_scene.py`** draws the person's real hardware where the task's grader located it, using the grader's own functions, never a search of its own: the two 608 bearings (catalog size) in the seats `wheel._seats` reads, pressed in flush from the open face as the R2/R3 failure drawing places them, with the M8 bolt through, the wheel stood on its edge with the axle level; the salvaged 1/4"-20 bolt in the bore `bolted._main_bore` picks (followed again with the failure drawing's `_bore_holes`), its head in the recess the grader reads, the clamp's wide face down and the camera side up; the ISO 4032 nut in the pocket `nuts._nuts_pocket` picks, turned the way `nuts._nut_in` fits it, with the bolt through, and the knob (which has no one way up) shown with the face the nut goes in from on top. The printed part keeps its own colour (orange) and the hardware gets steel, dark and zinc colours, each a layer that can be switched off. A design where the grader found no seat, bore or pocket, or whose wheel is in two printed pieces nothing says how to stack, gets no view and a short reason (`scene_none` in the manifest, shown under the picture).
- **Play the fit.** Each piece the grader decided carries its way in, how far it travels and the outcome, copied from the saved finding's words only: the wheel's R3 ("presses in and holds", "wouldn't take the bearing", "too loose and fell out"), the knob's R4 ("prints say holds / won't go in / falls out") and the plate's R3 with R4 ("goes through by hand", "won't pass", a head recess "too tight for this head"). It seats; it stops at the opening; or it seats, the part turns over so the opening faces down, and it drops out. Anything else ("loose" nobody turned over, "not measured", a bolt that only screws through, a size between prints) plays nothing and says so. The caption names the check and quotes its words, and says it is an illustration of the recorded test-print outcome for that size, not a simulation and not a print of this design. With prefers-reduced-motion the button shows the end state without motion. Where R3 passes only because the design's test print would find a size, the seat as drawn still stops, and the caption says why the check passed.
- **Estoa.** The LED disk was already drawn (see-through) in every Estoa scene; a test now checks it. The USB-C plug head is now drawn at the ESP32's port where R3 passed, at Dan's measured 11.58 x 6.44 mm (`catalog/connectors.yaml`, the size R3 reads), 30 mm long (the same "how far out" estimate `assembly_scene.PLUG` already used). The cable isn't drawn: no record says where it runs.
- **Left out.** The tripod's clamp jaws: nothing records them beyond the 38 mm plate they take. The bolt head (15 x 5 mm) is the person's answer and its 10 mm length the assumption R5 states, and the caption says so. The knob's bolt is a plain rod.
- **Pipeline.** `scripts/results_site_media.py` has `SCENE_VERSION`, so redrawing the assembled views doesn't re-render every thumbnail, and uploads 6 at a time by default (8 hit Cloudflare's rate limit). The viewer (`viewer.js`) plays the fit; `fit.js` holds the caption and button, so `detail.js` changes by a few lines.
- Tests in `tests/test_results_site.py`: the outcome comes only from the saved words; every built wheel, plate and knob design has a view or a reason; each fit's outcome matches its finding, on a unit axis, stopping short only when it stops; Estoa scenes show the disk and the plug only where R3 passed.
